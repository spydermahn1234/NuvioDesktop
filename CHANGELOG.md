- **Beat Grid: Kick, Snare And Hats Read On The Beat** - with the network's beat grid in place, the drum rows are now read on it: each sixteenth-note step of each bar measures how strongly the kick, snare and hats rose right there, and compares that step with the same step in the four bars either side, because a drum part repeats. A step lights when it is part of the pattern there and something sounded this time, or when it is as strong as the drum's real hits anyway (a fill). Hits stay on the frame they were played, so swing and pushed beats are not squared off. On a synthetic Billie Jean loop with its loud eighth-note bass the kick row went from 41 % right to 100 % (it missed 6 % of kicks), and the snare from 76 % to 100 %; with chord stabs on the off-beats the snare went from 47 % to 63 % right. Where the bass is mixed louder than the kick it is still mistaken for one. On real tracks the rows now show the songs' patterns (a hip-hop loop's kick on 1 and the "a" of 4 with the snare on 3; a clean 2 and 4 backbeat). Melody, and anything outside the beats the network heard, is unchanged; without the network the rows are read as before. Old maps are redone.

- **Beat Grid: Beat One In The Right Place (A Beat-Tracking Network)** - the beat finder decided where the bar starts from "kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4", which cannot tell beat one from beat three when the kick plays both (Billie Jean), and its kick and snare reading was shaky in a full mix (a real 130 BPM track gave 43 kicks a minute), so the bar line landed on the wrong beat. The grid now comes from Beat This! (JKU Linz, ISMIR 2024, MIT licensed), a small neural network trained on thousands of annotated songs that marks every beat and every downbeat itself; it hears the bass line and the chords as well as the drums. It runs once per track on the same background decode as the loudness measurement (about 3 s a minute of music on the test machine here, likely more on the HTPC since it uses two threads; the first grid comes with the first 15 s window and firms up as the rest is read). The grid is now the song's own beats bar by bar instead of one tempo laid over the whole track, so songs played by people no longer drift off it; a waltz gets bars of three (the fourth column stays dark) and a two-step is shown as bars of four at the beat you hear. If the network still puts beat one a beat out, press OK on the Beat Grid (or click it) to move beat one along; that choice is kept with the track. The kick, snare, hats and melody rows themselves are unchanged. The models (about 11 MB) ship beside the app in resources\beat-tracking, outside the app jar, and ONNX Runtime was already in the build for backdrop framing, so updates do not grow; without them the old estimate is used. Checked here on Creative Commons tracks against the original model (the bar line agrees with the network's downbeats on 69 of 69, 44 of 45 and 13 of 13 bars, tempos agree with librosa) and on a synthetic Billie Jean loop (all 30 downbeats on beat one); not yet heard on the real Billie Jean or on the HTPC. Old maps are redone.

- **Song Map: The Right Tempo And Bar Line** - the tempo finder only checked the beat itself, so busy hi-hats and syncopated kicks made a false pulse (5/4, 4/3 or 3/2 of the real tempo) look as steady as the real one (84 read as 105, 93 as 116, 104 as 130), and the bar position was wrong on about half the songs. It now checks that a tempo repeats at 1, 2, 3 and 4 beats (the bar), settles the grid over the whole track so it does not drift, and finds the first beat of the bar from kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4 and the sound changing at the bar line. Songs with no steady beat say so instead of guessing. Tracks under 67 BPM still read as double time, and very fast or trap songs can read as half time. Old maps are redone. Measured on made-up test songs only, not yet on real music or the HTPC.

A stream it cannot download (playlist streams, very large files) is read the old way. Windows build only; needs the new native player library.
- **Song Map keeps trying longer when the music server says 'too many requests'** - the logs show the server behind the music (HTTP 429, 'Too Many Requests') refusing the extra connection the Song Map's read uses while a song plays, so some tracks never got a map. The read now backs off 20, 40, then 60 seconds between tries and keeps going for 12 tries instead of 6.
- **Credits are saved in 16 small files instead of one 27 MB file** - every album the credits sweep finished rewrote the whole credits file (27 MB and growing), which is what made the disk and CPU busy for the first hour. They are now split across 16 files by album, so a save rewrites a sixteenth, and what the old file held moves over by itself the first time (the old file is then empty and can be ignored).
- **The AI limit is shared, so the filler and every feature now respect it** - Groq's free allowance (200,000 tokens a day) is spent by everything: song stories, track notes, Start here, Lineage, Sonic map and TV recaps as well as the background filler. The filler only counted its own tokens against its allowance, so it kept going after Groq was already full, and each feature kept asking too, one request per song, each answered with another 'rate limit' error (the log shows hundreds of them at 14:35 and 14:55). Now, once Groq says when to come back, no request is sent until then (the answer is the same error, instantly), and the filler waits for that time instead of counting it as failures and pausing 30 minutes. The filler's 'tokens used in the last 24 hours' now counts every AI request Nuvio made, not just its own.
- **Credits sweep no longer stalls the app** - checking which albums still need credits decoded every kept credit text in full (the credits store is now 27 MB; one album alone is 116 KB), once at start and every 10 minutes, and showed up in the log's "LaunchStall" reports. It now reads two fields off the stored text and runs off the main threads.
- **Song Map only part-filled for some tracks** - the background read that builds a track's map (and measures its loudness) opens its own second connection to the stream, and on your addon streams that connection was sometimes refused instantly part way in (your log: "track decode stopped at 135s of 340s", on about half the tracks), and the read then gave up for good, leaving the rest of the map empty. The read now uses windows of two minutes instead of one (half as many connections to the stream), keeps what it had read when the native 90 s limit stops a window, and a stopped read is now resumed from where it stopped, up to six times at 20, 40, 60 s... (the part already read stays on screen), and the map and loudness are kept once it finishes. The native side now also writes why a window failed to the log ("audio spot 135s+60s failed: ..."), so if some tracks still fail the log says whether it is a refusal, a timeout or something else. Not yet looked at on the HTPC; this changes the native player, so it needs the next full build.
- **Credits no longer wait for the AI** - the Music facts filler looked up each album's MusicBrainz credits in between its AI steps, so a Groq rate limit, a used-up daily allowance, a missing key or the 30-minute pause stopped credits too. Credits now run on their own track beside the AI facts: no AI is involved, so they carry on whatever the AI is doing (they still wait for a film and for your own lookups, and keep MusicBrainz's one call a second). Settings > Music > Music facts gets a line "Credits: N of M albums checked". Not yet looked at on the HTPC.
- **Who made it (and the song writers and producers) found nothing for almost every album** - two faults. (1) The lookup asked MusicBrainz for the recordings' relationships with the wrong option (`recording-rels`; a release lookup needs `recording-level-rels`), so it got no per-song credits at all, only the few album-wide ones (a mastering credit, say), which is why The Slim Shady LP showed no writers or producers. (2) It only looked at the first edition listed and then remembered "no credits" for 30 days. It now asks correctly, goes through up to five editions until one has per-song credits (keeping the biggest answer otherwise), and everything already kept is looked up again (credits are kept under a new key, `people3`, and the song-credit cache under `tcred3`). Checked by hand against the live MusicBrainz answer for The Slim Shady LP (writers Eminem, Dr. Dre, Jeff and Mark Bass, Royce da 5'9"; producers Eminem, Jeff and Mark Bass, DJ Head; engineer and mix credits); not yet looked at on the HTPC.
- **Your history counted every version of a song as one** - playing Virtual Insanity once also marked its Salaam Remi remix and its live take as played (and counted three plays). Songs are now told apart by their whole title, so each remix, live take or demo has its own count. The plain song keeps the plays it already had; the versions start from zero.

- **Automatic Subtitle Sync** - an optional setting under Playback → Subtitles (off by default). When an addon subtitle loads, Nuvio reads the timing of the subtitle tracks built into the stream and lines the addon subtitle up with them, fixing both a constant offset and the steady drift of a subtitle made for a different frame rate. Only a confident match is applied, and the original timing stays otherwise; a message says which happened. Styling, positions and ASS effects are kept. A subtitle Nuvio picks automatically may be swapped for a better-fitting one in the same language; one you pick is never swapped. Works on MKV and MP4 streams that carry their own text subtitle tracks (in any language), including torrents on either P2P engine (Nuvio Engine or TorrServer); there it waits until playback has a healthy buffer before reading, so it doesn't compete with the pieces the player needs. Streams without built-in subtitles, MP4 files whose only subtitles are images, and fragmented MP4 are left as they are. Idea is based on NuvioTV's AutoSync (by DavidVamaiotu, from Nuvio Reshaped).
- **Automatic Backdrop Framing** - the Adaptive and Adaptive Ambient heroes now crop each backdrop around what is in it, instead of cropping every backdrop at one height. A small saliency model finds the main subjects (cartoons included) and a face detector finds faces; the crop keeps as many of them as it can, with room above heads, hats and helmets, and leaves room on the left for the hero's title and details. It falls back to the old position for backdrops with nothing to go on. Each backdrop is analysed once, in about a tenth of a second, the first time it's shown; a new backdrop's fade waits for it, and the result is kept on disk. With Prewarm Artwork (Advanced → Cache) on, backdrops included, the hero's backdrops are analysed in the background as they're warmed, so they're framed from the first time you see them. That includes the rotating hero, the backdrop each focused card would bring up, and the Library's. It can't know which character matters most, so a lone figure at the top of an ensemble shot can lose out to a row of faces below. On by default; turning off Settings → Home Layout → Display Mode → Automatic backdrop framing brings back the Backdrop vertical position slider. Needs the Microsoft Visual C++ runtime, which most PCs already have; without it the slider's position is used. Adds about 11 MB to the install.

Shared by everyone on the PC: server choice, window position, updates, image cache and prewarm, local library, downloads and auto-download, games, screensaver, lights, external players and the P2P engine. ### Changed

- **Basic Hero Rotates Again** - the Basic hero carousel now moves on to the next title every 8 seconds, as upstream's does, and wraps from the last back to the first. Paging it yourself restarts the timer. It holds while a trailer is playing on it and while you're away from Home (in the player, Settings, game mode or the source picker).
- **Smarter MDBList Rating Refresh** - MDBList ratings are no longer kept for a flat week. How long each one is kept depends on where the title is in its release. An unreleased film waits until its cinema date. In its first cinema week it refreshes every 2 days, then waits for its streaming date. Its first four streaming weeks refresh every 3 days, then every 2 weeks, and about 4 months after streaming it is kept for 60 days. A show refreshes every 3 days while its first season airs and weekly while a later season airs. Off-season or ended, it is kept for 30 days, or until its next dated episode if that comes sooner. Each title also gets its own small extra delay of up to 20%, so titles cached in one evening don't all expire on the same day and eat one day's API limit. These are tuned for a free key. Settings → MDBList → Ratings Refresh has a Refresh Pace of Standard, Faster (half the wait) or Fastest (a quarter), for paid keys. Custom sets each interval separately. A change applies to ratings already cached, not just to new fetches.
- **MDBList Ratings In Bulk** - when a title's ratings are due but its keywords and release dates are still current, Nuvio now refreshes the ratings through MDBList's bulk lookup, up to 200 titles for one request, instead of one request per title. Lookups that arrive together, like a hero carousel's, share a request. Once Home is up, and every 6 hours after, ratings that have come due are refreshed in the background in batches of 200, leaving the last 100 of the key's daily requests for browsing. Keywords, the digital date and episode dates still come from the single lookup: weekly for about two months around release, every 90 days after, and with each refresh while a film in cinemas has no streaming date yet. If the bulk lookup fails, Nuvio goes back to single lookups for an hour. Refresh Pace has a new default, **Auto**: Standard on a free key, Faster on a supporter key, read from the key's daily limit. Explicit choices are kept. When MDBList rate-limits the key, titles now keep their cached ratings instead of showing none.
- **Anime Shader Setting Sticks** - setting the anime enhancement mode to Off or an Anime4K preset no longer goes back to Custom Shader after a restart. Anyone who used custom shaders before they became an anime mode had an old setting that forced Custom Shader again on every launch. It now runs once and is cleared.
- **RTX Video On Anime** - RTX Video Super Resolution now runs on anime that isn't using Anime4K, a custom shader or SVP. Before, any title detected as anime lost it, even with none of those running. RTX Video True HDR stays off for anime unless the new **True HDR on anime** setting (under RTX Video True HDR) is on, because its SDR-to-HDR pass can oversaturate flat anime colours. Neither ever stacks on Anime4K, a custom shader or SVP, but they now stay on while SVP is skipped at 1.5x speed or faster, where before playback ran with neither. Starting playback with RTX on also no longer rebuilds NVIDIA's video processor a second time.
- **Smaller Updates** - updating now downloads only the files that changed instead of the whole 220 MB zip. Nuvio reads the list of files at the end of the release zip, compares it with what is installed, and fetches just the changed files out of the same zip. An update that only changes Nuvio itself is now about 50 MB; one that also changes the player or P2P engine adds those files. Each file is checked against the zip's own checksum as it arrives. The whole zip is still downloaded (and checked against GitHub's SHA-256 as before) when automatic installing is turned off, when most of the release changed, or if anything about the partial download goes wrong. To make this pay off, TorrServer, the backdrop framing models, the anime ID list and the setup wizard's screenshots now ship as files in a `resources` folder beside Nuvio.exe, so updates don't download them again when they haven't changed.
- **Because You Watched Titles** - a "Because you watched" row could be named after an episode rather than its show (for example "Because you watched The Last One (2)" for Friends), because some watch history, including SIMKL imports, stores the episode title on each episode. Discover now names the row from the show itself.
- **Faster Home When An Addon Is Slow** - Home now loads catalogs four at a time and starts the next one as soon as any finishes. Before, it loaded them in groups of four and waited for the slowest in each group, so one slow catalog held up every row after it. In one case, three anime catalogs waiting on a shut-down service took a 43-row Home from about 2 seconds to 27. A slow catalog now only delays its own row. Any catalog that takes 3 seconds or more is named in `nuvio.log`.
- **Calendar Redesign** - the calendar now always shows six weeks, starting on your locale's first weekday, with days from the neighbouring months dimmed and past posters toned down. The month and its arrows sit centred above the grid, with Today beside them when you're looking at another month. Each day shows one +N badge for the number of other titles airing, and a tick when you've watched everything on it. A panel right beside the grid lists the focused day, replacing the pop-up: air time, a Series or Season premiere chip, a Finale chip (Trakt only) and Watched. A show's episodes on the same day are combined into one row ("S2 · E1–E8 · 8 episodes"). Keyboard: arrows move the focused day, carrying on into the next or previous month at the edges, and the panel follows; Enter moves into the panel (Left or Back returns to the grid); Home jumps to today. Ported from Nuvio Tracker.
- **One Download Per Image** - when the same poster is on screen in more than one place at once (the hero and a row, or the same title in two rows), Nuvio now downloads it once, and the other places wait for that download. Before, each place downloaded it separately at the same moment: in one session, 111 posters were each downloaded two to four times, and each download took the poster service about 2.5 seconds to render.
- **SIMKL Rewatches Keep The Original Watch** - while a SIMKL rewatch is active, finishing an episode now records it on that rewatch only. Before, the episode was also scrobbled to the show's original watch, which SIMKL then reported as restarted from the rewatched episodes (for example Friends showing 5/236 originally watched). Starting, pausing or seeking an episode during a rewatch also no longer reaches SIMKL: the playback session that opened was never stopped, and SIMKL logged it as a watch of the original when the next episode started, so the show jumped back to the top of SIMKL's Recently Watched as "Seen All" in place of the rewatch at every episode. As a result SIMKL won't show a rewatch as "watching now", and a rewatch episode you stop partway isn't saved to SIMKL's playback, so it won't appear in Continue Watching when SIMKL is its source. Turning Track SIMKL rewatches back on re-reads your rewatch sessions from SIMKL in full.
- **Lighter SIMKL Sync** - SIMKL Continue Watching and Plan to Watch are now kept on disk and updated with only what changed since the last sync, as SIMKL's sync guide requires. Before, both lists were downloaded in full on every launch and again after every episode you watched, which counted against SIMKL's daily request limit. Paused playback is only re-read when SIMKL reports a change to it, and titles removed on SIMKL are found with one small id-only check shared by every list, run only when SIMKL reports a removal. A write SIMKL turns away because another is still being processed is now retried instead of failing.
- **Watched State Across Id Types** - titles your metadata addon identifies by TMDB id (`tmdb:…`) and SIMKL history identified by IMDb id (`tt…`) are now recognised as the same title. SIMKL-imported episodes show as watched on those details pages, and marking a season or show watched no longer comes back from SIMKL as a second copy of every episode.
- **Bulk Watched Feedback** - marking a whole show, a season, or all previous episodes watched (or unwatched) now shows a toast saying how many episodes of which title changed, so an accidental click is noticed straight away. Every manual watched action, and every write to SIMKL's watch history, is now logged with the exact episodes involved.
- **Resizable Sources panel** - drag the in-player Sources panel's inner edge to set its width (up to three quarters of the player); it remembers your width, and double-clicking the edge puts it back to the default.
- **TorBox Store Node Hopping** - TorBox links on its newer `store-NNN…tb-cdn.io` servers now get the same recovery as the older `nexus` ones. When a server accepts the connection but never answers (seen as a minute-long hang ending in a failed open or resume), Nuvio retries the same link on a neighbouring TorBox server instead of giving up, and avoids the stalled server for 15 minutes. These links are also no longer probed before playback, and a debrid server that goes silent is given up on after 15 seconds instead of a minute, so the retry starts while you're still waiting.
- **Playback Failures In The App Log** - when a stream fails to play, `nuvio.log` now records the error (with any link cut down to its host, so tokens never reach the log) and what the player did next: refresh the link, fail over, or go back. Debrid links also no longer hand a failed open to youtube-dl, whose "not found" errors used to bury the real failure in the playback log.
- **SIMKL AUTH V2** - connecting to SIMKL now uses its new sign-in (OAuth 2.0 device flow): Nuvio opens simkl.com with the code already filled in, and sessions renew themselves in the background. SIMKL is retiring the old sign-in around April 2027, and its Client IDs can't be upgraded. Existing connections keep working until then. To move across, register a new app at simkl.com/settings/developer, paste its Client ID into SIMKL settings, and press Reconnect. Your watch history stays where it is.
- **Trakt Is Back** - Trakt API apps are free to create again, so the Trakt integration is supported again. Connecting now uses Trakt's device-code sign-in: Nuvio shows a short code and opens auth.trakt.tv/activate to approve it. Nuvio now ships its own Trakt app, so Connect works without creating one; a Client ID of your own can still be entered in Trakt settings. There's no client secret, which Trakt now deprecates for desktop apps, and no redirect URI, which Trakt's new rules don't allow to be `localhost`. Connections made before Trakt's October 2026 auth change can't be refreshed any more, so they show a Reconnect button, and your lists and history are kept.
- **Real File Name In mpv Stats** - mpv's diagnostics overlay showed `<hash>?token=<api key>` as the file name for debrid links, and later the source label. It now shows the media file name the addon reports (falling back to the label when it reports none). The request sent to the server is unchanged.
- **Keyboard And Controller On Who's Watching** - the profile picker now works without a mouse. It opens with your last profile focused; arrows (or the D-pad) move between profiles, Add profile and Manage profiles, Tab and Shift+Tab step through them, and Enter or A picks one. The first press shows a highlight on the focused card. Escape or B leaves Manage mode. Before, nothing on the screen took focus, so no key reached it.
- **Faster Hero Backdrops** - wherever the hero follows focus (Home, Discover, Library), the backdrops of the cards next to the focused one, and of the cards Up and Down would land on, are decoded ahead in the direction you're moving, so most moves show a backdrop that's already in memory (about 9 in 10 on Home, and Discover went from 1 in 4 to over 8 in 10). Outside Home only final artwork is decoded ahead. A backdrop only slightly larger than the hero is no longer resized on the CPU first; the graphics card scales it as it draws, and 4K JPEG backdrops decode at 7/8 size (24 MB instead of 33 MB) without losing sharpness. Title details for the focused row and its neighbours are also looked up ahead on every page, not only Home.
- **Lower Memory Use** - Nuvio's Java heap is now capped at 1 GB (it had no limit and could grow to 8 GB), and about 100 MB less of it is in use: the calendar and lightweight title caches are kept in files and read when needed instead of held in memory, Home's remembered row positions no longer keep scrolled-away rows (and their posters) alive, and Swing's window-sized back buffer and the tray icon no longer stay in memory. Starting playback or hiding the window also releases the graphics card's unused upload buffers (about 250 MB in playback), and memory is tidied up after heavy artwork browsing once you pause. Poster resizing now works in place instead of copying each image several times, which cuts the short-lived memory each poster costs.
- **Hero Trailers In Your Language** - the hero trailer now prefers a trailer in your metadata language. Trailer lists hold every language a site offers, and the first one was played whatever it was.
- **Details Open Faster From Cast And Browse Pages** - opening a title from a cast member's page or a badge or company list waits at most 150 ms for its IMDb id, then opens with its TMDB id instead of waiting for the lookup.
- **Optimisation Pass** - browsing the app is significantly smoother. Going back from a details page is instant, leaving playback is faster, long Home pages with many rows scroll more smoothly, details pages load faster, and the player controls and stream list respond quicker. Settings pages now load piece by piece instead of a whole category at once, which also stops them causing stutter during playback.
- **PC Right-Click Menu** - a proper desktop right-click menu, now used everywhere the old basic white menu appeared.
- **Glass Skip And Next Episode Popups** - the skip intro and next episode popups are now glassmorphic to match the Minimal player layout.

## 1.5.0 - 2026-06-23

### Added

- Added **SIMKL integration** - connect via Settings → SIMKL using a PIN code (no password required). Once connected, SIMKL can replace or supplement Trakt across the following areas:
  - **Scrobbling** - watch progress is reported to SIMKL in real time alongside Trakt. Both services receive start/stop events simultaneously.
  - **Library** - enable "Use SIMKL as Library" to show your SIMKL watchlist (plan-to-watch) as the app library, split into My Shows, My Movies, and My Anime sections.
  - **Continue Watching** - enable "Use SIMKL for Continue Watching" to drive the CW section from SIMKL playback sessions. In-progress items and up-next suggestions both come from SIMKL, with images fetched from your installed addon providers. Items can be dismissed (calls `DELETE /sync/playback/{id}`). A configurable day cap (14 / 30 / 60 / 90 / 180 / 365 days, or all time) prevents old watches from flooding the list. CW entries are sorted most-recently-watched first.
  - **Calendar** - enable "Use SIMKL for Calendar" to populate the existing calendar screen with upcoming episodes from shows you follow on SIMKL. Fetches episode schedules directly from each show you are watching or plan to watch, so smaller shows that don't appear in the global CDN calendar are still included.
- Added **standard Anime4K presets** - Mode A, Mode B, and Mode C (Fast and HQ variants each), ported from the official [bloc97/Anime4K](https://github.com/bloc97/Anime4K) v4.0.1 preset chains. These join the existing Stremio-Kai presets (Optimized / Fast / HQ) in the F10 cycle and Fork Enhancements settings. Mode A is best for blurry/compressed sources, Mode B for already-clean sources, Mode C for heavily noisy or compressed sources.
- Added **auto-apply toggle** for Anime4K - the "Auto-apply to Anime" switch now appears below the preset picker whenever a preset other than Off is selected. When on, the chosen preset is applied only when the title is detected as anime; when off, the preset is always active regardless of content. Previously this behaviour was buried inside the single "Auto" mode with no way to choose a specific preset for auto-detection. Existing "Auto" users are automatically migrated to Optimized + auto-apply enabled.
- Added **local file drag-and-drop** - drag a video file from Explorer and drop it onto the Nuvio window to open it in the player immediately. Supports mp4, mkv, avi, mov, wmv, flv, webm, m4v, mpg, mpeg, ts, m2ts, mts, vob, ogv, 3gp, rm, rmvb, and more. Progress tracking and scrobbling are disabled for local files as we don't have the IDs.
- Added **NVIDIA RTX Video True HDR** (Settings → Playback → NVIDIA RTX VIDEO). When enabled, the player's NVIDIA video processor converts SDR content to HDR in real time using AI-based tone-mapping. Requires an RTX GPU and Windows HDR enabled in Display Settings. Off by default. HDR and Super Resolution can be active simultaneously. Requires a libmpv build from February 2026 or later - this release bundles an updated libmpv (master, June 2026) that includes the required `IMGFMT_X2BGR10` output format and `ID3D11VideoContext1` colour-space interface. Previous releases could not implement this feature: mpv 0.41 accepted the `nvidia-true-hdr` option but the VP kept an 8-bit output surface so the driver never engaged.

### Fixed

- Fixed **AVI files playing with sound but no video**. The player was attempting D3D11VA hardware decoding for all codecs (`hwdec-codecs=all`) while also disabling the software fallback (`vd-lavc-software-fallback=no`). For AVI containers - which often carry codecs D3D11VA doesn't support (MPEG-4 Visual / DivX, or H.264-in-AVI with non-standard header extradata) - hardware decoding would silently fail with no fallback, killing the video track while audio continued. `hwdec-codecs` is now restricted to codecs D3D11VA actually supports on Windows, and the no-fallback restriction is removed entirely.
- Fixed **continue watching tracking stopping after pause/resume**. When the generation check inside `emitTraktScrobbleStart`'s async path failed (due to a concurrent state change), it exited without resetting `hasRequestedScrobbleStartForCurrentItem`, permanently blocking all subsequent start events for that session.
- Fixed **final episode progress not reaching scrobble services**. When the 80% completion threshold fired mid-episode, `hasSentCompletionScrobbleForCurrentItem` blocked the video-end stop from sending - leaving services showing "5 minutes remaining" instead of complete. The final stop now always fires at ≥99% progress.
